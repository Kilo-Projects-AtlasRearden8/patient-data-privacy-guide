# Patient Data Privacy Checklist

Print this checklist and keep it at your station. Check items off as you complete them. All examples are fictional.

Created by Asdale Dynamics, LLC — educational only, not legal advice. Contact your privacy officer for workplace-specific rules.

---

## A. HIPAA basics

- [ ] I know what counts as protected health information (PHI): names, IDs, dates, diagnoses, photos, billing info, and anything that could identify a patient
- [ ] I know PHI exists on paper, on screens, and spoken out loud
- [ ] I follow the minimum necessary rule: I only view or share the PHI I need for the task
- [ ] I only access patient records for patients I'm involved in caring for — never out of curiosity
- [ ] I never look up family, friends, neighbors, or celebrities' records
- [ ] I don't discuss patients in elevators, hallways, cafeterias, or other public areas
- [ ] I know my organization's privacy officer or compliance contact (name saved in my phone)
- [ ] I know my organization's incident-reporting process for potential breaches
- [ ] I know I should report a privacy mistake right away, not hide it

## B. What never to paste into AI chatbots

- [ ] I never paste real patient names, initials, IDs, MRNs, or account numbers into any AI chatbot
- [ ] I never paste patient birth dates, admission dates, discharge dates, or appointment dates
- [ ] I never paste diagnoses, test results, medications, or treatment details tied to a real patient
- [ ] I never upload photos of patients, wristbands, charts, or screens
- [ ] I never paste screenshots of the EHR or other clinical systems
- [ ] I never use personal AI accounts for work tasks involving patient information
- [ ] I only ask AI general questions ("What are common side effects of lisinopril?") — never about a specific real patient
- [ ] If I use AI for patient education drafts, I remove all patient identifiers myself
- [ ] If I accidentally pasted PHI into a chatbot, I report it to my supervisor or privacy officer immediately
- [ ] I ask my privacy officer before using any AI tool with patient information, even local models

## C. Securing work devices

- [ ] My computer, phone, and tablet lock automatically after 5 minutes or less of idle time
- [ ] I lock my computer (Win + L on Windows, Control + Command + Q on Mac) every time I walk away
- [ ] I never leave a logged-in work device unattended
- [ ] I use a different strong password (14+ characters or a random-word passphrase) for each work account
- [ ] I store work passwords in an approved password manager — never on sticky notes
- [ ] I changed default passwords on all new devices before first use
- [ ] Multi-factor authentication is turned on for my work accounts where available
- [ ] I install operating system and app updates when IT makes them available
- [ ] I only install organization-approved software on work devices
- [ ] I connect work devices to secured organizational Wi-Fi, not public Wi-Fi
- [ ] I use my organization's VPN when accessing work systems from home, if provided
- [ ] I never log into work accounts on shared or public computers
- [ ] I send PHI only through secure, organization-approved channels
- [ ] I never forward work email to a personal account
- [ ] I never text PHI through consumer messaging apps unless my organization specifically approved it
- [ ] I know to report a lost or stolen work device, badge, or phone to IT immediately
- [ ] I return all work devices, badges, and keys when I leave or change roles
- [ ] I never keep patient information on personal devices, USB drives, or personal cloud accounts
- [ ] I give old devices to IT for secure wiping — never the trash
- [ ] I put printed PHI in a shred bin, never the regular trash

## D. Safe information sharing

- [ ] Before sharing PHI, I ask: Does this person need it for this patient's care? Is it the minimum necessary? Is the channel secure?
- [ ] I share PHI only with care team members who need it for treatment, payment, or operations
- [ ] I verify the identity of anyone who calls or emails asking for patient information
- [ ] I call back through a known number, never a number given by an unknown caller
- [ ] I send PHI through secure organizational email, secure EHR messaging, or approved encrypted platforms
- [ ] I never send PHI through personal email or consumer texting apps
- [ ] I confirm fax numbers before sending, and use a cover sheet
- [ ] I never post about work or patients on social media, even without names
- [ ] If someone asks me to access or share patient information outside my role, I say no and direct them to the proper process
- [ ] I know patients have a right to their own records, and I never pressure a patient to share information

## E. If something goes wrong

- [ ] I report lost or stolen devices to IT immediately
- [ ] I report possible PHI exposure (wrong email, lost paper, chatbot paste, snooping) to my supervisor or privacy officer right away
- [ ] I don't delete, alter, or "fix" records after an incident
- [ ] I don't notify patients myself unless my privacy officer tells me to
